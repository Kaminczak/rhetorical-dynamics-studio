**How the Schema Bridges the Core Frameworks**

Rhetorical Stance & "They Say / I Say": The schema evaluates how effectively a text establishes a "They Say" backdrop before introducing its thesis
. It tracks whether the text agrees, disagrees, or adopts a nuanced "agree and disagree simultaneously" position
. It also checks for the integration of a preemptive "naysayer" to build credibility
 and the presence of concession-rebuttal pivots
. For academic and professional texts, it tracks structural transitions according to John Swales' "moves" in Genre Analysis (e.g., establishing a territory, identifying a niche/gap)
.
Syntax and "Rhetorical Grammar": The schema analyzes sentence variety across syntactic distributions (simple, compound, complex, and compound-complex clauses)
. It evaluates voice choices (active vs. agentless passive), ensuring passive structures are used strategically to background or conceal agency rather than as grammatical errors
. Most importantly, it grades the text's flow against the "Given-New" cohesion contract, ensuring sentences start with contextually established information and end with new focal details
.
Vocabulary Tiering & Adaptability: Rooted in Beck and McKeown's three-tier framework
 (adapted for the Common Core by Marilee Sprenger
), the schema classifies word distributions into Tier 1 (basic), Tier 2 (general academic), and Tier 3 (domain-specific)
. It flags opportunities for pedagogical "recoding"—where difficult, high-frequency academic verbs (such as analyze
 or delineate
) are bridged using student-friendly, concrete metaphors without sacrificing conceptual accuracy
.
Actionable Revisions: To ensure the tool is highly prescriptive, it generates a list of proposed rewrites. Each recommendation requires mapping the verbatim text to its rewritten form, selecting a focal area, identifying the specific theoretical framework, and providing a concise, professional rationale for how the syntactic or structural shift alters register, tone, and authority.
