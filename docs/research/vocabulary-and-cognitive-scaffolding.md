Foundational Distinction: Tier 2 vs. Tier 3

**Tier 2 Words:** High-frequency, general academic terms that appear across diverse subject areas (e.g., *analyze, delineate, summarize, establish*). These are indispensable for mature reading comprehension and test readiness.**Tier 3 Words:** Low-frequency, highly specialized, domain-specific words that are crucial to understanding core conceptual content in specialized fields (e.g., *crust, stoichiometry, photosynthesis*).



Rule 1: Maintain the Target Word, Adapt the Scaffold (No Dilution)

The most common mistake in early grade bands is substituting a simpler synonym for a Tier 2 word (e.g., asking elementary students to "find differences" instead of using the word *contrast*).

**The Rule:** **Never replace the target term with a simplified synonym during core instruction.** Doing so denies students exposure to high-stakes terminology they will encounter on assessments.**The Scaffold:** Teach the exact word (e.g., *delineate*) explicitly from the beginning, but adapt the definition’s complexity to the grade level. For example, in elementary grades, *stanza* is defined simply as "a fancy poetry word for paragraph", whereas in high school, it is taught through structural and rhyming analysis.

Rule 2: Follow a Developmental progression of Cognitive Demand

Vocabulary instruction must align with a student's cognitive maturity. Sprenger charts a systematic grade-band introduction for academic verbs:

**Elementary (K–3):** Focus on foundational comparative and descriptive actions, such as *compare, contrast, describe, distinguish, identify, retell* (K); *demonstrate, determine, draw, explain, locate, support* (Grade 1); and *comprehend, develop* (Grade 2).**Middle School (4–8):** Scale cognitive demand to inferential and meta-textual verbs, introducing *infer, integrate, interpret, paraphrase, summarize* (Grade 4); *analyze* (Grade 5); and *articulate, cite, delineate, evaluate, trace* (Grade 6).**High School / AP (9–12):** Focus on complex, multi-source synthesis (*synthesize* is introduced in Grade 11), requiring students to process and blend conflicting viewpoints to create new meaning.

Rule 3: Use the "Recoding" Loop (Formal → Informal → Formal)

To prevent cognitive overload, students must temporarily translate concepts into their own words, a cognitive process called **recoding**.

**The Rule:** Allow students to use informal definitions and personal metaphors during their first exposure to a term. However, this informal translation must act as a temporary bridge, not a replacement. Lessons must systematically loop back to the formal target word to cement it in long-term, automatic memory.**Actionable Strategy:** In pairs, elementary students might explain *interpret* as "decoding a secret message" or "reading facial expressions", while high school students construct formal concept maps relating *interpret* to translation and text evidence.

Rule 4: Leverage Multi-Audience AI Prompting

Generative AI can translate abstract Tier 2 and Tier 3 vocabulary into grade-appropriate language without losing conceptual accuracy.

**The Rule:** When prompting an AI to simplify vocabulary, utilize **dual-prompting constraints** that define the precise target audience and context.**Example (Adapting "Artificial Intelligence" via Skrabut):***Prompting for Elementary (7-year-old):* Instruct the AI to explain the term using concrete, physically grounded metaphors (e.g., comparing AI to "rules a computer uses when you play a video game on a phone").*Prompting for High School (16-year-old):* Instruct the AI to introduce abstract classifications while anchoring them in concrete frameworks (e.g., distinguishing between "weak AI" like virtual assistants, and "strong AI" like self-driving systems).

Rule 5: Align Vocabulary Tasks with Bloom’s and Webb's Taxonomies

Adapting vocabulary across grade bands is not just about changing definitions; it is about changing **what students do** with the words.

**The Rule:** Frame the vocabulary tasks to match the cognitive depth expected of that grade band.**Task Scaling:***Remember/Understand (Elementary):* Identify a *metaphor* or *simile* in a song.*Apply/Analyze (Middle School):* Analyze word choices to determine *tone* and *mood*.*Evaluate/Create (High School/AP):* Evaluate an author’s *argument* by assessing their *evidence*, or *synthesize* multiple historical texts to form a unique research thesis.

Rule 6: Implement the "Always Validate" Quality Gate

When utilizing AI models to simplify highly complex Tier 3 domain-specific terms for younger grades, there is a severe risk of the model creating misleading metaphors or over-simplifications that dilute the scientific or mathematical truth.

**The Rule:** **Apply a rigorous human-in-the-loop review.** Every AI-generated translation must pass a baseline academic gate to ensure the core mechanics of the concept remain sound. If a metaphor obscures the physical or mathematical reality of a term, reject or refine the prompt iteratively until the core structure is mathematically and factually precise.
